## Challenges

1. **Story 8.3 concentrated all debugging**: Three distinct tooling issues (export extensions, BodyInit cast, vitest alias) hit in the same story. These were infrastructure concerns, not business logic — but they took time.
2. **Export map extensions not anticipated in spec**: The `.mjs`/`.d.mts` fix was a known pattern but not documented as a prerequisite in the 8.3 story spec. A "known tooling gotcha" section in stories touching both packages would have prevented the surprise.
3. **Scope expansion during review (8.3)**: `InitiateUploadError` + `ReconcileError` were a valid correctness fix, but touched 8 files across packages. This extended the story's change surface beyond its nominal scope.
4. **Story 8.1 used Sonnet 4.6, not Opus**: The Opus rule for Epics 6–9 was set for quality assurance. 8.1 was trivial enough that Sonnet was appropriate in practice, but the deviation should be a deliberate exception, not a habit.
5. **Documentation actions from Epic 7 retro still unverified**: Action items #2 and #3 (project-context.md) were not confirmed completed before Epic 8 started.

## Key Insights

1. **Formalized triptyque eliminates dormant bugs**: The discipline established as a structural habit (task in spec, not just team memory) proved its value immediately. Zero bugs survived to the next story.
2. **Tooling gotchas compound in cross-package stories**: Stories touching both `core` and `adapters` expose infrastructure issues invisible in single-package stories. These deserve explicit "known gotchas" sections in their specs.
3. **Reviews are the right place to catch semantic consistency**: The UploadError union gap was a design-level issue, not a code bug. Review caught it. The fix was worth the scope expansion.
4. **Documentation debt is the recurring weak spot**: Three consecutive epics have had documentation action items not fully completed on schedule.
